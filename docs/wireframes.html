S-01 Settings

Where the app connects to Meta and Resend, and where the sending rules live. Spec §5.2.

  1. 1
    Connection bannersThese show on every screen until a channel is connected. Previews always show Mock mode.
  2. 2
    Write-only secretsSaved values are encrypted and never shown again. A value supplied by an env var reads "set by env var" and takes priority.
  3. 3
    Test connectionCalls Meta and shows the number, verified name, quality rating and messaging limit, or the error.
  4. 4
    Webhook cardEverything to paste into Meta's dashboard (§7B Step 7). The last-event time confirms the webhook is working.
  5. 5
    Resend key sourceThe status says where the key came from: .local.bin, env var, Settings, or not set.
  6. 6
    Domain verificationLists the DNS records to add. Email campaigns stay blocked until the domain is verified.
  7. 7
    Postal addressRequired before the first email, because it goes into every email footer.
  8. 8
    Sending rulesEach channel has its own limits. The wireframe groups them side by side for easy comparison; the spec lists them per channel.
  9. 9
    Team allowlistOnly these emails can sign in. All team members have equal access, and changes are audit-logged.
  10. 10
    DataRestore an earlier contact version, export contacts, and manage opt-out keywords and suppressions. A restore can never re-enable an opted-out contact.

S-02 Import: choose and map

Steps 1 and 2. Both are shown together here; in the app they are separate steps. Spec §5.3.

  1. 1
    Instant previewThe browser previews the first 200 rows while the file uploads directly to Blob, so large files avoid the 4.5 MB limit.
  2. 2
    Detected settingsEncoding, delimiter and header are detected automatically, and each can be overridden.
  3. 3
    Duplicate file warningShown when the file's SHA-256 matches a committed import.
  4. 4
    PresetsShared across the team and applied automatically when the headers match.
  5. 5
    Column mappingEach column maps to a standard field, a custom field or Ignore. Headers are auto-suggested, so "Mobile No" becomes Phone.
  6. 6
    Column statsHow full and how unique each column is, to help spot the right one.
  7. 7
    Amber: invalid emailA warning only. The row is still imported, without the email.
  8. 8
    Red: invalid phoneThis row will be rejected. Here, Excel has damaged the number.
  9. 9
    Separate consentWhatsApp and email opt-in are set independently, from a column or for the whole file.
  10. 10
    Phone is requiredNext stays disabled until a column is mapped to Phone.

S-02 Import: validate and result

Steps 3 and 4: see what will happen, then commit. Spec §5.3.

  1. 1
    Whole-file summaryThe server checks every row and compares it with the current contacts. Nothing has been saved yet.
  2. 2
    Opt-outs are permanentAn import can never switch an opted-out or suppressed contact back to opted in, on either channel.
  3. 3
    Existing-contact policyDecides what happens to rows already in the store. Tags are always merged.
  4. 4
    Rejected rowsDownloads rejects.csv with a reject_reason column, so the list can be fixed and re-imported.
  5. 5
    Consent checkboxesRequired when importing as opted in on that channel. The person and time are recorded. The proof itself stays outside the app.
  6. 6
    Result cardShows the final counts and the new store version, and links to the imported contacts or a campaign.
  7. 7
    UndoReturns to the previous version. Suppressions are re-applied afterwards, so opt-outs survive.
  8. 8
    One import at a timeOnly one commit runs at once. Anyone else sees who holds it.

S-03 Contacts list

Find an audience and act on it. Empty state: "No contacts yet", with a link to import. Spec §5.4.

  1. 1
    Sending bannerShows on all screens while a campaign runs, with one line per running channel.
  2. 2
    Search and columnsSearch matches any part of a name, phone or email. The column chooser adds custom attributes.
  3. 3
    FiltersAlso covers import batch, date created, email suppressed and attribute equals. Every column can be sorted.
  4. 4
    Select all matchingSwitches from a page of rows to "this filter, minus these exclusions", so selection doesn't depend on paging. The selection stays in the URL, ready to share.
  5. 5
    Bulk action barSend campaign is the main action and opens S-07 with the channel preset. Each bulk change creates one new store version.
  6. 6
    Consent per channelTwo separate badges, each overlaid with its suppression list, so an opt-out shows before the Parquet file catches up.
  7. 7
    Open a contactClicking a row opens the S-04 drawer.
  8. 8
    Server paging100 rows per page, with shift-click for ranges. Selection and phone columns stay visible when scrolling sideways.

S-04 Contact drawer

Everything about one person, opened from a row in the contacts list. Spec §5.5.

  1. 1
    Drawer, not a new pageSlides over the list, so the filters and selection stay in place behind it.
  2. 2
    Same validation as importPhones and emails are normalised the same way. A phone that belongs to another contact is rejected.
  3. 3
    One row per channelA WhatsApp STOP leaves email consent unchanged, and an email unsubscribe leaves WhatsApp unchanged.
  4. 4
    Removing a suppressionAsks for a reason, which is audit-logged. A complaint entry needs an extra confirmation.
  5. 5
    All channelsThe one action that opts someone out of both channels at once.
  6. 6
    Combined historyMessages from both channels, with status, timestamps and any error or bounce reason.

S-06 WhatsApp designer

Build a marketing template and send it to Meta for approval. Spec §5.7.

  1. 1
    NameLowercase letters, digits and underscores, typed names are auto-slugified. Category is fixed to MARKETING and language to English for now.
  2. 2
    Variable styleNamed is the default. Positional variables must run in order from 1.
  3. 3
    HeaderOptional: text, image (5 MB), video (16 MB) or PDF (100 MB). Files upload directly to Blob.
  4. 4
    BodyUp to 1,024 characters with WhatsApp formatting. The variable menu suggests contact fields. It can't start or end with a variable, because Meta rejects that.
  5. 5
    SamplesRequired for every variable, since Meta needs them to review the template.
  6. 6
    ButtonsUp to 10, with quick replies grouped together. A "Stop promotions" button also opts people out automatically.
  7. 7
    Live validationErrors block submitting; warnings don't. For example, missing opt-out wording is a warning.
  8. 8
    Submit to MetaDisabled, with the reason, when no credentials are set. Drafts always work. In Mock mode, templates are approved for testing.
  9. 9
    Live previewUpdates on every keystroke, using the sample values.

S-11 Email designer

Build a marketing email from blocks. No outside approval is needed. Spec §5.12.

  1. 1
    SubjectRequired, and can use variables but no HTML. A marker shows how much fits in most inboxes.
  2. 2
    PreheaderThe hidden preview text beside the subject. Leaving it out gives a warning.
  3. 3
    BlocksHeading, Text, Image, Button, Divider and Spacer. Drag to reorder, up to 30 blocks.
  4. 4
    Image rulesUp to 1 MB, and alt text is required. A missing alt blocks Mark ready.
  5. 5
    Fixed footerBusiness name, postal address and unsubscribe link. It can't be edited or removed, because the sender rules require it.
  6. 6
    Live validationErrors include a button without https and HTML over 100 KB, where Gmail starts clipping. Warnings cover spam risks such as all caps.
  7. 7
    Test and readySend test uses the sample values. Mark ready needs zero errors. Running campaigns keep their own snapshot, so later edits never change an email mid-send.
  8. 8
    Three previewsDesktop, mobile and plain text all update as you type.

S-12 Unsubscribe page

A public page with no sign-in, reached from an email. Three states are shown. Spec §5.13.

  1. 1
    Confirm firstOpening the link changes nothing, because mail scanners open links automatically. The address is masked.
  2. 2
    The button unsubscribesIt adds the address to the email suppression list and logs it. Gmail's one-click Unsubscribe does the same without opening this page.
  3. 3
    DoneRepeating it is harmless and shows the same message. WhatsApp consent isn't affected.
  4. 4
    Invalid linkA tampered link writes nothing and shows the reply-to address instead. The wording here is a placeholder; the spec only calls it a generic page.

Deliberately minimal, accessible, and free of tracking scripts.

S-07 Campaign wizard

All steps are drawn on one board; in the app they run one at a time. Opened from Contacts or Campaigns. Spec §5.8.

  1. 1
    Six stepsThe steps adapt to the channel chosen first.
  2. 2
    ChannelPreset from the Contacts split button, and fixed once the campaign is created.
  3. 3
    Exclusions with countsShows exactly who is removed and why. Email adds: no address, email suppressed and duplicate address.
  4. 4
    Unknown opt-inExcluded by default. Including these contacts needs a toggle and an acknowledgement, and is audit-logged on email.
  5. 5
    TemplateOnly APPROVED MARKETING templates, or READY email templates, are offered.
  6. 6
    VariablesEach maps to a field, attribute or static value, with a fallback. Recipients still empty are listed, then skipped.
  7. 7
    PreviewStep through real rendered messages, or search for one contact. Email also shows the inbox line, HTML and plain text.
  8. 8
    Limit checkCompares recipients plus the last 24 hours with Meta's tier, and offers to split. Email checks the daily send cap, and blocks sending without a key, verified domain or postal address.
  9. 9
    Test and timingTest goes to the numbers in Settings and isn't recorded in the campaign. A send outside the window waits until it reopens.
  10. 10
    Typed confirmationSend stays disabled until the count is typed. Recipients are then frozen, and suppression and the frequency cap are re-checked before each send.

S-08 Campaign monitor

Follow a campaign live and step in when needed. Spec §5.9.

  1. 1
    HeaderName, template, status, who started it, when, and the ETA.
  2. 2
    ControlsAny team member can use them, and each is audit-logged. Pause takes effect within about one batch.
  3. 3
    Auto-pause bannerExplains why the circuit breaker stopped the campaign. This banner is an example; a campaign shows Running or Paused, not both.
  4. 4
    Live countersAccepted only means Meta queued the message; webhooks confirm delivery. Delivered and read also show as a percentage of accepted. Polling drops to every 30 s after the run, since webhooks keep arriving for days.
  5. 5
    Email versionDifferent counters, rates that turn amber then red near the thresholds, and clicks per link. Opens are approximate because of Apple Mail Privacy Protection.
  6. 6
    Failures tableError code plus a plain explanation, exportable as CSV. Email shows the address, bounce type and Resend error.

Resend unknown needs an explicit choice. These messages may already have reached people, so the app never resends them by itself.
